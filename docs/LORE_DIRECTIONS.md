# The Procession — lore integration proposals

**Status: Direction A selected by the user on 2026-10-25; original proposal comparison written 2026-10-24.** Directions B/C are retained as historical alternatives. The accepted premise is recorded in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md); [eight collectible drafts](LORE_COLLECTIBLES.md) develop it for review. Detailed ontology, names, final specialty mapping, and incidental draft details remain open. Source IDs below resolve in [LORE_RESEARCH.md](LORE_RESEARCH.md).

## Scope and approach

Use a small, forgotten event within the established world's history. The four are not existing named characters, their relatives, or a secret cause behind the principal story. Any historical workers, witnesses, and settlement introduced here are original supporting material. Existing characters need not appear, endorse the explanation, or provide quests.

The primary foundations are the handbook's rift/Rust World connection (L1); accounts of mechanical helpers and bells used for recording and rescue (L8–L10); and recorded engineers' explanation of bells, the Chamber, and resonators (L16). Human suffering and ordinary work provide the emotional setting (L7, L9, L18–L20). None of these establishes the existence of our quartet.

## Direction A — a rescue call that never ended (selected)

### Central idea — original addition

At a forgotten underground shelter, an ordinary maintenance crew adapted available bell and resonator technology to keep contact with workers beyond a dangerous passage. Their apparatus repeated a call and waited for an answer before the passage could be declared clear.

The final operation failed during a temporal disturbance. The far end of the link was lost. Something now returns through heavy storms: four walking forms that still perform the abandoned work of calling, listening, and keeping a way open.

The four are **otherworldly remnants of that apparatus and its work**. They need not be four transformed humans. Cloth, bindings, ironwork, and human proportions suggest what was once around the equipment; they do not settle what is inside the bodies. Whether the remnants retain purpose, merely repeat a procedure, or imitate a remembered rescue remains a worthwhile player-facing uncertainty.

They keep a way open so that help can reach the missing. The beings that answer are dangerous, and the quartet gives no sign of recognizing the mistake.

This invents the shelter, apparatus, accident, and resulting creatures. Canon supplies the technological and social context, not proof that this happened or that sound ordinarily sustains temporal passages. Avoid naming this a standard, widely deployed technology.

### Why four different forms

Proposed roles describe parts of one operation, giving each silhouette a reason to exist. The first three family assignments remain proposals; these roles are not new gameplay abilities.

| Existing visual / working label | Proposed role in the lost apparatus | Present expression | Proposed specialty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Veiled figure, recessed face, mouth grille | The caller: carried a recognizable voice through the passage so lost workers could find the route | A human-like call stretched into an unbroken, unreadable chant; the recess need not hide a human face | Bowtorns |
| Plated figure, cage-like head | The tender of mechanical helpers: relayed instructions to equipment working beyond sight | Plates and cage look more like the remains of a working assembly than armor worn for combat | Locusts |
| Bound figure, hunched with elongated arms | The holder: kept the far apparatus braced while people and equipment passed; bindings resemble stays and carrying slings | A posture of permanent strain, as if still bearing a load that cannot be seen | Shivers |
| Open U-shaped tuning-fork head | The listener: supplied the reference against which the other parts could recognize a reply | A body organized around listening rather than a mouth; it accompanies the one bell brought out at setup | One opening bell, plus the agreed drifter pool |

The locust association has a particularly useful historical echo in accounts of small mechanical helpers. The bell association follows recorded communication/rescue functions. **No reviewed text gives bowtorns or shivers corresponding historical jobs.** Their response to the caller/holder must remain an original link, not a claim that bowtorns were messengers or shivers were porters. In our proposal, different parts of the damaged call reach different creatures. Why those particular creatures answer can remain unresolved in collectibles.

All four can draw drifters because those beings also answer the overall call. This does not establish that drifters are the former workers, or that any responding family was created by the apparatus.

Do not use this fiction to settle portal lifetime, absence/reload handling, multiplayer scope, cross-source specialist shutdown, or bell-child restrictions. Those remain separate open design cases. No precise lore explanation is necessary for every balance number.

### Canonical support, pressure points, and questions

**Support:** bells' former rescue/recording role is directly represented in multiple texts (L8–L10). The engineer's account connects recording, a repository, retrieval, and synchronization (L16). The handbook explicitly connects rifts and Rust World conditions (L1). A maintenance crew in a minor refuge fits accounts of machinery, shortages, and ordinary underground labor (L7, L9, L19–L20).

**Pressure points:** recorded sound preserving information does not establish that sound can hold a rift or acquire a body. Those are deliberate additions. The engineer describes the Chamber as a receptacle, not a listening mind: do not turn it into a conscious entity, make it the storm's creator, or have it send these beings. We need not attach the accident to the canonical Archives or use any of its inhabitants. The shiver/bowtorn links have no independent historical support yet.

**Open questions:** were these originally walking mechanisms or did their bodies arise only after the accident? Are they trying to rescue anyone, or repeating an operation without understanding? What counted as a successful reply? Should evidence establish the lost apparatus while leaving the actual nature of the four unresolved? Do we prefer this proposed silhouette mapping?

### How future collectibles could reveal it

These are artifact roles, **not finished collectible drafts**:

- An original stores clerk's requisition places cloth stays, metal cages, a bell, and tuning equipment together without explaining the creatures.
- A different worker's account shows why the rescue arrangement mattered personally and gives no theory of the Rust World.
- A damaged local diagram makes four complementary positions recognizable but does not depict a cult or promise a boss.
- A later survivor's field note records that killing one restores the others; its proposed explanation can be wrong.
- A separate observer notices the opening bell and repeated call, recognizing an old work signal without identifying what now performs it.

All authors, locations, and their exact knowledge would be planned before writing. The set should support the local history and practical warning without giving one narrator the whole explanation.

## Direction B — native beings that make our world habitable for them

**Original addition:** the four are inhabitants or extensions of the Rust World. Their coordinated activity makes a temporary foothold persist on this side. Human-like cloth, cages, and braces are things incorporated into their forms rather than evidence of human ancestry. The fork-headed being uses a bell as something it recognizes or can employ, without being its inventor.

**Support:** rifts connect the worlds (L1), and the bell's installed mixed roster demonstrates a pre-existing association among the defender families at the configuration level (A1). This supports encounter compatibility, not a confirmed ecology.

**Distinct payoff:** their storm is their shelter. From the player's perspective they are destroying stability; from their perspective they may be maintaining it. The four functions could be complementary organs of one distributed being, explaining restoration after a part is lost.

**Inventions and tension:** the ecology, distributed body, repair process, and ability to use bells are all invented. We must not define the entire Rust World, assert a known home society, or equate every existing monster with one species. The visual references' manufactured materials need a convincing incorporation history. Their reason for four forms remains less historically grounded than in A.

**Open questions:** are they conscious, are defenders attracted or directed, and are human forms camouflage or the result of contact? Recommend this direction if the user wants the quartet to feel fundamentally alien rather than tragic remnants of human work.

## Direction C — the involuntary recurrence of a lost rescue party

**Original addition:** four ordinary people once died during an underground recovery attempt. Heavy storms repeat the event in altered bodies. Their chant is a broken work response; the defenders enter because the disaster itself is recurring. These are impressions of people, not existing lore protagonists and not necessarily souls or resurrected originals.

**Support:** the game's documents preserve personal loss, evacuation, rescue failures, and differing memories (L7, L9, L18, L21). Temporal disturbances and the Lens account permit an uncanny setting (L1, L3), but do not prove literal event replay.

**Distinct payoff:** individual human stories and the relationship between the four could be more prominent. Their appearance can preserve equipment, bodily strain, and clothing from the last task they attempted.

**Inventions and tension:** physical temporal impressions, repeated deaths, and survivor reconstruction would all be additions. The Chamber's remembered sounds do not establish replayed bodies. This risks overexplaining supernatural creature origins or making human identity too certain; it also needs care to explain why a replay can be interrupted and why the dead do not return during that encounter.

**Open questions:** are these people remembered by the world or things wearing the memory of people? Would identifying four biographies reduce the intended otherworldliness? Recommend this only if the user prefers personal tragedy over alien ambiguity.

## Recommendation and discussion boundary

Direction A has the strongest combination of specific existing lore connections, four understandable silhouettes, and independence from the main cast. Establish a forgotten rescue operation in our mod's history; leave open what has returned from it. Its emotional center is ordinary people depending on a call being answered, and the horror is an answer that no longer means rescue.
