## P1 — A Voice in the Rain

*Individual note: the Veiled. Traveler's journal.*

Ed insists it was saying something. He has written the words on the opposite page, though he has crossed them out twice already.

I heard a long, low noise. There might have been words in it. There might have been words in the rain, too, if I had stood there listening long enough.

The thing wore a covering down to its knees. Its face was set so far back that my lantern showed only the little bars across the mouth. Ed called to it. It continued making its noise. He went nearer.

That was when the bowtorn above the bank put a shaft through his pack.

I pulled him down into the ditch and we crawled until the bank hid us. The covered thing had neither moved toward us nor raised a hand. I could still hear it after we reached the road.

Ed is angry about the pack. He says I dragged him away before he could understand.

Tomorrow I shall give him mine. Perhaps then we can stop arguing about which of us nearly got him killed.

## P2 — An Item of No Value

*Individual note: the Resonant. A repairer's account to a business partner.*

Marta,

You will find the pry bar missing. I know what you will say. I have had it these eleven years and managed to bring it home from every foolish undertaking until this one.

There was a figure standing beyond the charcoal pit, all loose plates and rust. A cage where the head should be. Nothing in its hands. Naturally I supposed somebody had left a machine there, and naturally I went to see whether any part of it was worth carrying.

I put the bar under a plate at its side. The plate gave a little. The noise inside changed, and three locusts came scrambling over the bank.

I left the bar where it was.

From the fence I could see them gathering about its feet. The figure paid them no more attention than it had paid me. It went on humming with my good iron sticking out of it.

Charge the loss against my share. And if anyone offers you plates with that green staining in the seams, let him unload them in somebody else's yard.

## P3 — Correction to the Northern Survey

*Individual note: the Bound. A surveyor's annotation accompanying a sketch.*

The mark beside the old lime workings does not indicate a hoist. I have amended the drawing, though the first impression was understandable.

In the poor light I took the arms for hanging ropes, and the bent back for the frame from which they hung. There were bands around it, drawn tight enough to disappear between the folds. I could not see what they supported.

I watched from the cutting. Twice I was certain it was about to straighten. It remained bent, making a sound I felt through my teeth.

A shiver came up from the workings and forced me to leave my instruments. I recovered them after the storm. The ground beneath the figure bore no weight that I could account for: no fallen beam, no stone, no machinery. Only flattened grass.

I have drawn a burden across its shoulders in the margin. That is my attempt to make sense of its posture. No such burden was visible.

Anyone copying this sheet should omit it. A conjecture has a way of becoming a landmark once it is put on a map.

## P4 — For the Next Watch

*Individual note: the Hollow. Instructions left by a watchkeeper.*

The tall one has two prongs above its shoulders. There is open sky between them. I mention this because Beren's drawing makes a square of it, and he has been telling everyone to look for a man wearing a box.

You will see it with the other three when the bad storms come. A bell comes with them. You can hear that well enough from here.

We thought the bell was giving the others their orders. Beren and I went down while Hessa watched the path. It cost him a spear, but we broke the thing and got back with only a cut on my leg.

The four were still sounding when we reached the lookout. They continued until the fog hid them. Killing the bell had done nothing that either of us could see.

I cannot tell you what will stop them. Hessa says we should try the tall one next. Hessa can go herself, provided someone else takes her watch.

There are clean bandages in the lower drawer. Leave some for whoever comes after you.

## P5 — Until They Answer

*Group note: original purpose. An uncollected instruction for a relief shift.*

To the relief foreman,

We have lost the lower party. I mean contact with them. Please make that distinction when you speak to the families.

The bell was working when they passed the last bend. We heard the carts, then someone asking for another lamp. Since then we have had nothing we can make sense of. Twice we sent a man to the bend. Both returned to us without finding it, though neither remembers turning.

Keep the caller sounding. They know that voice. Leave the tender at its work, since the little machines may yet find a route our men cannot. The brace must remain under load, and the listener must be kept clear of the floor. Those four stations are to be attended until we have an answer.

I have been asked how long this arrangement can hold the way. I cannot give a useful answer. The masons will no longer agree on the length of the passage.

There are six people down there. Pell has the bad knee. Sella will have given away her portion by now. Their names are on the ration board, which someone has already begun to wipe clean.

Leave the names where they are. I shall speak to the stores myself.

Oren

## P6 — The Lower Party

*Group note: later interpretation and a warning. A letter accompanying copied work records.*

I have enclosed the foreman's instruction because I think we have misunderstood what happens at the workings.

The four make their noise, and creatures come. That much we have all seen. We have supposed this means they command them. The old paper suggests another possibility. Someone once kept a way open there and called for help. Perhaps what we hear is that call continuing.

The hunters allowed me to accompany them last time. They had wounded two of the figures before bringing down a third. I watched a split close in the plated one's side. The other injured figure stood whole again as well. The fallen one remained on the ground.

They finished the survivors one at a time. Even the last kept sounding. When it fell, the distant iron shapes vanished and we could see the ridge properly again.

That is what I witnessed. The rest is my reading of a frightened man's instruction.

If I am right, they may still be waiting for those six people. Every creature that approaches may be another answer they cannot understand. I do not know whether anything remains in them that could be told to stop.

Send a copy to the households along the northern road. Whatever the four intend, our people must know what happens when one falls.

## P7 — Relief Overdue

*Archive-exclusive transcript. Four voices; attribution to the work stations is provisional. No date recovered.*

**Caller:** Please send someone to relieve us. We have kept the instructions. I have repeated the message slowly and left room for an answer. There has been none. I tried the passage behind us, but after a while I could hear myself ahead. The others say I never went out of sight. I do not want to try it alone again.

**Tender:** The little machines come back with their feet full of the same red dust. I cleaned one and sent it along the wall with a length of cord. The cord came back before the machine. I know how that sounds. I have laid them both beside the bell so that whoever comes can see. Please bring someone who understands the workings. I am afraid to take anything apart now.

**Holder:** I can keep the brace in place. Tell them that first. I let it ease a little when the others were away, and there was a noise beyond the bend. Somebody coughing, I thought. I put the weight back. I should have called out to them. I was frightened they would hear me and ask how much longer.

**Listener:** I have heard the request for another lamp several times. The same scrape before it, the same breath after. I think it is the old request coming through again. I have not told the others. They stop being afraid for a little while when they hear it.

**Caller:** Tell the relief to bring a lamp of their own. Ours gives enough light to see the wall, but I cannot remember what colour it ought to be.

**Holder:** And a second set of stays. Please. Someone must take the weight before I can come away.

## P8 — An Answer Received

*Archive-exclusive transcript. Related passages retrieved separately; intervals between them could not be established.*

**Caller:** We used to call their names. Someone decided it would be kinder, so they would know we had remembered. I still have six places in the message, but some of the names are gone. I leave a little space where each should be. Perhaps they can put their own name there when they hear it.

**Tender:** There is something wrong with my hands. I know the movements for undoing a clasp. I can remember doing it, but when I look down I cannot find where to begin. The others must have taken my tools. I have asked them to give them back. They pretend they have not heard.

**Holder:** The stays have grown into something. There is no place to put a finger underneath. I asked for a knife, then remembered what it was I meant to cut. I have stopped asking. When the weight shifts, the others cry out, and afterward none of them will admit it.

**Listener:** I told them about the lamp. I told them we had been listening to an old voice. They asked how old. I could not say. Then they asked how old my voice was. I have been listening for a new sound ever since.

*[A later passage.]*

**Tender:** Something came when we called. It dragged one foot. The others would not let it near. Pell had a bad leg. I tried to tell them. I cannot remember which leg it was, and they have made that an excuse to send it away.

**Listener:** There are answers now, if I listen long enough. A scrape can be an answer. Feet coming toward us can be an answer. We were wrong to expect them to speak clearly after so long down there.

**Holder:** I used to think someone would take my place. I would rest, and afterward I would come back to help. There must have been somewhere I meant to go while I rested. I cannot think of it now. Keep the others here. I cannot bear this alone.

**Caller:** I have made the message shorter. The names were upsetting everyone. I say that the way is open. I say we are waiting. When something comes, I begin again, so that those behind it will hear us too.
