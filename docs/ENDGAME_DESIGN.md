# The Far Relay — final boss and event control

**Development update, 2026-10-29:** the later user instruction authorizes actual development. See [IMPLEMENTATION.md](IMPLEMENTATION.md) for the implemented alpha: three compact landmarks, a simpler 420-HP melee/pulse Custodian, and earned Closing Bell control. The detailed galleries and lane/recall moves below remain a future design target. The original proposal text is retained as provenance, not a description of finished gameplay.

**Design discussion, 2026-10-29.** The user requested a distant temporal ruin, a final boss, and an earned object that lets the player choose whether the Procession event happens. The user explicitly selected **world-wide control**, with ordinary temporal storms continuing.

That direction is accepted. The names, site, boss, reward appearance, placement rules, and detailed interactions below are a recommended proposal for review. No implementation, game-ready assets, or gameplay testing exists.

## The endgame promise

**Travel to the last surviving isolation station, defeat its custodian, and bring home the means to quiet the Procession.**

The recurring encounter now leads toward an optional conclusion with a lasting practical reward. Players who enjoy the event can keep it available or restore it later. Choosing peace is as valid as continuing to hunt its relics.

This adds a separate destination to the expansion. The Lower Relay and its Relief Engine remain an earlier exploration chapter and optional miniboss. The final boss is encountered at the distant ruin; killing the four storm chanters never summons it. Their final death still immediately ends their current storm.

## The place: the Far Relay

A forgotten stone-and-timber station stands well outside the starting region. Its fallen surface structure conceals a larger facility whose machinery still answers a signal from somewhere else. The architecture is recognizably practical, but shadows lag behind the moving parts and dust hangs where a shaft should have fallen.

This was a remote isolation station associated with the lost rescue operation. It could separate one troublesome connection from the rest of the apparatus. Its existence does not make the operation the source of all temporal storms or establish a world-spanning canonical institution.

### Make it a real expedition

Recommend one intended final destination per world, at a fixed distant location associated with the starting region, rather than a ruin continually repositioned away from the player's current base. The journey should justify an expedition with supplies, staging stops, and a return trip. Exact distance, terrain suitability, map scale, and world-generation frequency remain open; no vanilla travel-time claim is made.

The Lower Relay contains a separate route board indicating the Far Relay. Finding it reliably reveals the destination's general location and bearing. Where map markers are available, an optional rough marker can support the journey; equivalent written directions are needed for mapless play. Records P5/P6 remain unchanged.

Discovery must not depend on obtaining all four 15% signatures, a rare navigation drop, or the Archives-only records. A player who finds the final ruin organically may enter it. The route board gives direction and context, not an arbitrary key.

### A proposed route through the ruin

| Space | Experience | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Fallen approach | Broken guide-posts lead toward a roofline partly buried in the hillside | Makes the location identifiable from outside |
| Relief quarters | Abandoned bunks, empty supplies, and a work roster end at an unfinished shift | Gives the journey a human scale before the boss |
| Return gallery | A navigable passage has delayed mechanical sounds and displaced shadows | Establishes temporal disturbance through presentation |
| Isolation workshop | A diagram shows one particular signal being admitted or isolated | Teaches what the eventual control object does |
| Service alcove | A sheltered staging point and an obvious retreat route | Lets the player prepare and recover without an endless attrition corridor |
| Receiving hall | Four inert reference instruments face a large sealed mechanism and its custodian | Houses the deliberate boss encounter |
| Quiet chamber | The protected isolation instrument and a final work instruction remain after victory | Guarantees the endgame reward and a restrained finish |

Use the existing proposed ruin enemies in finite placements on the approach. The receiving hall does not begin an unlimited defender stream. Ordinary storms can still occur during the expedition; the ruin itself does not manufacture or permanently lock a heavy storm.

The four reference instruments are machinery, not extra chanters or resurrected members of the quartet. P7–P8 remain found only in the Resonance Archives. No copy, playback, or paraphrased substitute for their undiscovered testimony appears here.

## The final boss: the Last Custodian

**Working name and nature are proposed.** A towering upright service frame remains at the isolation mechanism, still performing its last assignment. It treats an approach to the controls as interference. Whether anything experiences that obligation is left uncertain.

### A different silhouette from the Relief Engine

Where the Engine is a low hauling machine, the Custodian is a tall, broad frame: heavy iron-and-timber uprights, a deep slatted central housing, spread bracing feet, and two unequal arms. One arm ends in a wide support plate, the other in a narrow recovery cleat. The dark interior remains difficult to read. Brief temporal afterimages fall slightly out of step with its actual body.

Its materials connect it to the worksite: dirty linen repairs, worn pins, old markings, rust, and tiny verdigris seams. Its upper housing stays blunt and architectural; the Hollow retains the distinct open tuning-fork head.

The guardian is not a confirmed former foreman, one of the six missing workers, or the intelligence commanding the quartet. It protects the instrument that can isolate their signal. That relationship supplies a practical reason to fight it without explaining away the creatures.

### Begin on purpose

At first the Custodian remains braced around the mechanism. Approaching the final control or attacking it starts the encounter after a clear physical and audible warning. The player can inspect the hall and choose when to commit. A hidden conversation, lore-completion flag, or particular signature weapon is not required.

The fight uses one health pool. The three acts below describe a developing moveset, not three full health bars. Exact HP, damage, armor, thresholds, attack intervals, and multiplayer scaling remain undecided.

| Action | Tell | Response and opening |
|---|---|---|
| Hold the Line | The support plate draws across the body before a broad frontal sweep | Move around the committed arc; attack while the plate returns |
| Return Stroke | A narrow floor trace appears before the recovery cleat strikes along it | Leave the marked lane; the cleat stays down briefly |
| Bracefall | Feet spread and the central housing lifts before a short, heavy downward press | Leave the marked patch; the exposed side remains reachable during reset |
| Recall | A visibly faded trace marks where a recent strike will repeat | Avoid the old lane rather than chasing the afterimage; the present body remains the damage target |

The arm poses and traces need to be understandable in ordinary lighting, with sound and optional captions as additional cues. The afterimage is visibly different from the damaging body. It never silently duplicates the hitbox.

### Act I — keep the post

The Custodian stays near the mechanism and uses deliberate sweeps, straight strikes, and the downward press. The player learns the rhythm and how to move around the broad frame. Each committed action leaves time to hit back.

### Act II — the mechanism repeats

Proposed transition around two-thirds remaining health. After selected ordinary attacks, a faded floor trace warns that the previous strike will repeat in its original lane. The echo does not track a player who has already moved.

Limit overlapping threats so a visible escape lane remains. The challenge is reading a remembered action alongside the present body, rather than filling the room with damage. The boss remains vulnerable; there are no mandatory four-switch immunity phases.

### Act III — relief overdue

Proposed transition around one-third remaining health. The frame loosens from its original station and advances into the hall. Its old routines begin to fail: a sweep overextends, one brace drags, and long recoveries expose the interior. The Custodian has greater reach across the room but fewer clean defenses.

The final act should feel like the work outlasting the machine. Avoid replacing all the learned tells with faster versions. There is no renewed full heal, second summoned boss, or arena-filling defender wave.

On defeat, the Custodian folds against its own supports. The reference instruments fall silent. The player can enter the quiet chamber and recover the isolation instrument.

## The reward: the Closing Bell

**Working name and form are proposed.** A compact, placeable isolation instrument: an old metal bell body enclosed in a square timber-and-iron cradle, with four small tuning plates and one broad two-position lever. It looks like a worksite control taken home, rather than a new wearable relic.

The reward is guaranteed by victory. Recommend one world-shared, recoverable item in the protected chamber, available immediately after the boss is defeated, without another random roll or unannounced crafting requirement. The chamber is part of the obvious exit path rather than a hidden treasure hunt.

It is separate from the proposed 15% chanter signatures and the Relief Engine's Brace Maul. All four signatures may still be pursued before or after this expedition.

### Two clear choices

| Lever mode | World-wide result |
|---|---|
| **Procession allowed** | Future heavy storms use the ordinary configured Procession chance, currently 100% |
| **Procession suppressed** | Future heavy storms contain no Procession; ordinary temporal storms and their normal systems continue |

The accepted scope is world-wide. There is no protection radius or requirement to build one instrument at every home. Suppression blocks the mod encounter, not all rifts, ordinary storm monsters, environmental instability, weather, or temporal storms.

The instrument isolates this particular returning call. Its world-wide reach is original mod fiction, not a claim about canonical bell technology. Operating it does not prove the missing workers were rescued, destroy the Chamber, or erase the quartet from history.

### Recommended operating rules — still proposed

- The first recovered instrument defaults to **Procession allowed**. Placing it does not silently change the world's event availability.
- While placed, the lever can freely switch between the two modes. Once earned, this choice requires no fuel, charges, upkeep, repeat boss kill, or calendar expiration.
- The selected mode remains effective with its chunk unloaded, the owner offline, and across save/reload. It is not a proximity aura.
- Read the control when a heavy storm begins, before committing the procession's arrival. That storm keeps its initial encounter decision; toggling afterward takes effect at the next heavy storm.
- Suppressing while a procession is already present does not delete its creatures, cancel its storm lock, award victory loot, or end that storm. Existing chanter deaths and despawn recovery retain their ordinary rules.
- Allowing during an ordinary storm that began suppressed does not create a late procession. Repeated lever use cannot create multiple encounters or force a fresh storm.
- Picking up or breaking the installed controller restores **Procession allowed** for future storm decisions. The next placement begins allowed and displays that state clearly. Mode changes do not revise an already committed storm.
- Support one installed controlling instrument per world. Any replacement or duplicate must resolve to that same authority, rather than let two levers assert contradictory settings.

These timing and removal recommendations make the reward a choice about future encounters. The user has confirmed world-wide scope, not yet these particular edge-case rules.

Use plain interaction feedback: **Procession suppressed world-wide. Ordinary temporal storms continue. Applies to the next heavy storm.** The allowed state should be equally explicit. Appearance, lever position, text, and sound communicate the same mode without relying on color alone.

If multiplayer is supported, the device changes a shared world condition. Recommend an owner/server-authorized control rule, with a clear read-only state for other players. Exactly who may place, move, or operate it remains open; selecting world-wide control does not settle the older encounter-scope questions.

### A reward the player can recover

Recommend keeping the defeated state and unclaimed instrument persistent, so leaving or dying before collection does not lose the reward. Once claimed, a loss-recovery interaction at the cleared chamber should restore access if the control item is irretrievably lost. A replacement must supersede the old binding instead of creating a second independent controller.

Exact loss handling, pickup behavior, replacement eligibility, and duplication protection remain design work. The intended outcome is that a hard-won ability to control the event cannot become permanently inaccessible through an ordinary item-loss accident.

The Custodian does not automatically respawn. Reclaiming the reward may require a return to the cleared chamber, but never another boss victory. A repeatable challenge version, if desired later, is separate from the guaranteed control unlock and carries no implied repeat loot.

## The emotional finish

An original instruction beside the instrument can read:

> Isolate the return before leaving the station. If the next shift has not arrived, leave these instructions where they can be found.

This is a new draft, not a recovered canonical quote. It describes a procedure and an overdue relief shift without telling the player what the quartet really is.

The player can place the Bell beside the memorial board, close its lever, and know that another heavy storm will arrive without the Procession. They can also open the lever again. Quiet is earned, and continuing the encounter is a conscious choice.

## What changes in the existing design

- The baseline remains heavy-storm-only and currently 100% **while the event is allowed**. The earned world-wide control adds an explicit exception to the earlier unconditional trigger.
- Light and medium storms never gain the encounter. Allowing it does not summon one on demand.
- Four passive chanters, their health/resistance/healing, the eight shared defender slots plus one opening bell, reinforcement pauses/pacing, and last-death storm termination remain unchanged whenever the encounter occurs.
- The Lower Relay remains a regional exploration chapter; the Far Relay is the new final destination. The Relief Engine remains a miniboss; the Last Custodian is an additional proposed enemy.
- The expanded pitch now has **eleven enemy concepts**: eight procession defenders, Empty Stretcher, Relief Engine, and Last Custodian. It has **seven ruin/site archetypes**: six regional types plus the Far Relay.
- V5's reward figures apply to encounters that actually occur and qualify. Suppressed storms produce no procession kills or completion rewards. The Closing Bell is a separate guaranteed endgame reward.
- P7–P8 remain Archives-only. All new site/boss/control lore is original proposal material.
