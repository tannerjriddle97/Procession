## The expansion in one page

**The rescue never ended.** Four remnants of a failed relief operation emerge during heavy storms, still keeping a passage open for six missing workers. Their calls bring the wrong answers. The player learns how to break through those answers, discovers the ordinary people behind the abandoned operation, and turns recovered rescue equipment into useful survival tools.

The expansion should make a heavy storm feel like a dangerous field expedition. Preparation matters, sound and silhouettes convey useful information, and a retreat can still bring knowledge or a worthwhile partial reward. Between storms, small ruins lead toward a larger maintenance site and the consequences of an instruction nobody ever withdrew.

The proposed package contains:

- The existing four-chanter encounter, with more readable presentation, three formation layouts, and broader reinforcement choices.
- **Eight new defender variants** distributed across the four existing creature families.
- **Three additional ruin enemies**: Empty Stretcher, Relief Engine miniboss, and Last Custodian final-boss proposal.
- **Seven ruin/site archetypes**: six regional relief-site types including the Lower Relay, plus the distant Far Relay final destination.
- The existing **four proposed signature relics**, dressing, and lamp, plus **twelve new practical item designs**, four cosmetic pieces, and a separate proposed Closing Bell control reward.
- An optional investigation connecting the ruins, the eight existing collectible drafts, and new minor records.
- Rescue-themed building pieces, aftermath details, and a modest memorial outcome that does not resolve the quartet's nature.

The content has three rhythms: everyday preparation, a short and intense storm assault, and slower exploration after the weather clears. Each should improve the others. Exploration teaches an enemy tell; a storm supplies resources; recovered tools make the next journey more manageable.

## What gives it an identity

**Everything once had a useful job.** A shield began as a brace, a weapon as a cutter, a threatening machine as a relief winch. Recognizable hinges, cloth, oil cups, tally boards, and carrying frames make the supernatural failure feel physical.

**The horror comes from continuing the work.** A porter clears space for a stretcher that is empty. A light-bearing machine illuminates a route nobody should follow. A caller leaves room for an answer that never comes. Enemies need practical, readable behavior as well as unsettling appearances.

**Knowledge produces confidence.** The player recognizes the sound before the charge, learns which approach exposes a bowtorn, and chooses which chanter to reach first. The expansion should become more understandable without becoming harmless.

**Victory leaves something useful at home.** Relics matter in combat; tools support travel; salvaged fittings furnish a workshop. A player who ignores historical notes should still enjoy the encounter, while a reader finds a connected local tragedy.

The quartet are not confirmed transformed humans, the answering creatures are not confirmed missing workers, and the Chamber is not assigned consciousness. The original lore's gaps remain useful. The expansion develops the consequences of the rescue, rather than providing an authoritative explanation of the world's supernatural systems.

## The encounter, enhanced

### Preserve the fight's spine

While the event is allowed, the DLC keeps one procession during every heavy storm at the current 100% baseline chance, and none during light or medium storms. The earned world-wide control lets the player suppress Processions while ordinary temporal storms continue. Four passive chanters retain about 40 HP each and 50% ranged resistance. A death fully heals living chanters without reviving the dead.

The drain sequence remains **3x / 2x / 1.5x / 1x** ordinary storm drain with four / three / two / one chanters alive. The last survivor sustains the storm, and its death ends it immediately. There is no summoned finale or further objective needed to end that storm.

The opening remains one shiver, one bowtorn, one locust, one drifter, and the single additional bell. Eight living defenders share the allowance; the opening bell is outside it and never replaced. New defender variants occupy those same eight slots. All replacement sources, including bell children, obey the same kill-triggered pause and fresh 3–5-second per-spawn wait. Kills can extend the remaining pause, never shorten it, and delays do not add.

All-tier eligibility remains. The new creatures below have no approved HP, damage, armor, tier distribution, or kill-pause formula. Their descriptions identify tactical roles, not balance measurements. The existing family-to-chanter mapping remains a working proposal except for the Hollow's bell identity.

### Give each chanter a readable presence

| Figure | Proposed animation and sound | What the player can read |
|---|---|---|
| Veiled | Cloth draws inward on a long breath; a narrow grille gives a thin, sustained call | A tall, narrow silhouette and recognizable upper voice |
| Bound | Bindings tighten while the body leans against an unseen load; low wooden strain under the chant | A hunched, wide silhouette with hanging arms |
| Resonant | Small plates lift and settle in sequence; a dry mechanical pulse under the voice | A cage head and broken, angular shoulders |
| Hollow | The open U-shaped fork trembles along its inner edges; a spare, wavering reference tone | Open sky between two prongs; distinct from the separate bell |

These are presentation additions, not attack windups. The chanters do not lunge, cast damage, apply new debuffs, or gain aggressive phases.

When one dies, its sound drops out. A brief line of light and synchronized change in the survivors' posture accompany their full heal. The fallen body remains down. A short caption can say that the surviving forms have recovered. The four-part sound gradually becomes a lonely final voice; the last death restores ordinary world sound as the storm ends.

The restoration cue must communicate a complete heal. It should not suggest damage can be spread efficiently between chanters, or imply a visible projectile can be intercepted to prevent healing.

### Vary the ground plan

Three proposed formations change approach angles without changing quantities, rewards, or the storm rules:

| Formation | Arrangement | Tactical question |
|---|---|---|
| The Watch | Loose crescent around the arrival point | Can you use terrain to approach one end while keeping the far ranged defender screened? |
| The Brace | Two forward figures, two behind, with a clear passage through the middle | Do you enter the passage quickly or work around the flank? |
| The Search | An uneven line, as if attending a lost route | Which end gives a manageable escape path? |

Every figure needs a reachable approach. A terrain validation failure falls back to a valid arrangement rather than forcing the chosen formation. These are suggested placement patterns, not a claim that the unresearched vanilla rift rules support them already.

No formation creates walls, breaks player blocks, places the quartet inside sealed rooms, or requires a special key. Exact distances and spawn placement remain open. The proposal does not settle the initial four empty defender slots or portal lifecycle.

### Broaden the roster without increasing the crowd

Introduce eight distinctive reinforcements as variants within the existing families. Ordinary family members remain eligible. For an initial content balance target, allow **at most two of these eight special-behavior variants alive among the eight counted defenders**. That is a proposed composition limit, not an additional allowance; all sources share it. A failed special selection uses an eligible ordinary defender rather than creating a delayed bonus spawn.

Keep the mandatory opening roster on recognizable ordinary family members for the first presentation pass. The new variants enter later reinforcement choices. No new defender summons children, increases the cap, heals chanters, prevents storm termination, or creates another storm anchor.

The particular variant mix must still obey any family shutdown rule eventually approved. A dead specialist cannot be bypassed by secretly relabeling its creature as a drifter or a bell child.

## Eight new procession defenders

### M1 — Linekeeper, a drifter variant

A thin drifter carries a crooked guide-pole with a torn strip of cloth. It tries to stand between the player and the nearest chanter, jabbing from a little farther away than an ordinary close attacker. It creates a moving obstacle rather than a new damage spike.

**Tell:** the pole turns sideways before a straight thrust. **Answer:** step across the thrust, approach from another side, or interrupt it with a committed hit. Its long recovery gives the player a reason to press forward. It has no protective aura or ability to make a chanter invulnerable. The pole is part of the creature, not a guaranteed weapon drop.

### M2 — Burdened Porter, a drifter variant

A broad carrying frame rises above its shoulders. The porter walks slowly, then commits to a short shove that displaces the player only a small distance. Its job is to disrupt an overly static attack position, not launch someone across a hillside.

**Tell:** the frame rocks back with a creaking strap. **Answer:** move aside, let it overshoot, and use the open lane. No chain stun, long-distance knockback, automatic fall damage, or ability to pass through a shield is proposed. Carrying debris does not establish that drifters were historical porters.

### M3 — Seamrunner, a shiver variant

A narrow, uneven shiver moves along the edge of the group before making a brief ground-level dash. It punishes staring at one target and rewards checking the retreat route. The dash needs a traversable route; it cannot teleport, phase through a wall, or emerge behind the player without an approach.

**Tell:** a scraping foot and a pronounced sideways crouch. **Answer:** face the approach, sidestep the committed line, or keep rough terrain between you and it. The runner pauses after its burst. It cannot dash repeatedly until the player loses control.

### M4 — Pallbearer, a shiver variant

A broad-backed shiver drags two empty carrying rails. It performs a slow, sweeping strike that briefly hinders movement if it connects. The rails scrape loudly enough to identify it through fog.

**Tell:** both rails lift before the sweep. **Answer:** back out, circle during recovery, or interrupt the windup with a suitable heavy hit. A proposed short hindrance cannot stack or refresh into permanent restraint. There is no grapple, kidnapping, equipment theft, or forced transport. The rails suggest the rescue theme without making the creature a confirmed former rescuer.

### M5 — Bankside Caller, a bowtorn variant

A bowtorn with a projecting rib-like sight attempts to keep a bank, tree, or rock between itself and a direct rush. It takes ordinary shots from an exposed angle, then spends time relocating. Its distinctive challenge is positioning rather than unusually high damage.

**Tell:** a high, hollow scrape before it settles to shoot. **Answer:** advance under cover while it moves or force it out by changing approach. It cannot see or fire through solid terrain. The name describes its current behavior, not a historical occupation or a second chanter.

### M6 — Rackback, a bowtorn variant

An asymmetrical rack rises behind the head. The Rackback releases a short, clearly telegraphed sequence of weaker projectiles rather than one surprise heavy shot. Every projectile in the sequence must fit a bounded total attack budget; three arrows must not secretly mean three full ordinary attacks.

**Tell:** the rack spreads with a dry ratchet before firing. **Answer:** hold cover through the complete sequence, then push during the long reset. Projectile visibility and an optional caption make the volley readable. It has no explosive shot, homing ammunition, or ground hazard that persists after the fight.

### M7 — Windlass Locust, a locust variant

A squat machine has a drum-shaped body and stiff hauling limbs. It lowers itself, extends one short striking arm, and snaps it back. The attack suggests a failed recovery winch; it does not tether the player to the creature.

**Tell:** the drum turns twice before the arm strikes a visible narrow lane. **Answer:** step outside that lane and attack during the rewind. It cannot pull the player across terrain, move placed blocks, or trap someone in machinery. Its back silhouette stays rounded, reserving the open tuning-fork shape for the Hollow.

### M8 — Lantern Locust, a locust variant

A low machine carries a shuttered, dirty light. It sweeps the light along the ground while circling, then stops to make a short close attack. The light lets players locate the creature and creates an unsettling suggestion of a guided route.

**Tell:** the shutters close before the lunge. **Answer:** recognize that closing light, keep a little space, and strike while the shutters reopen. The beam does not blind the player, drain stability, reveal someone through a wall, or erase torchlight. It is part of the creature's presentation; it provides no portable lamp or limitless fuel when killed.

### Keep the combinations fair

The two-special composition target permits a Linekeeper and Rackback to make a guarded lane, or a Porter and Seamrunner to encourage movement. Avoid stacking displacement with repeated movement hindrance. Every behavior needs a visible recovery window.

The shared reinforcement pause still pauses spawning, not the attacks of already living enemies. New tells must make that distinction understandable. Defender kills remain valuable because they create time to reach a passive target; they should not become a demand to kill every guard before progressing.

Numeric tuning should compare each variant against its own family and selected tier. A stronger behavior must not receive an unexamined bonus in HP, damage, armor, and spawn weight simultaneously. Its pause reward should reflect actual threat when that formula is designed.

## A rescue landscape to explore

The world-content proposal starts with six regional relief-site archetypes and adds the Far Relay as a seventh, distant final destination. A player can discover the regional sites in any order. Repeated small sites vary their arrangement, supplies, and minor records; the larger Lower Relay ties their functions together and points toward the final expedition. Treat this as a failed operation with an isolated remote station, rather than a vast secret organization inserted into every part of the setting.

Sites should offer a recognizable clue from outside: a broken guide-post, a timber brace, an unusually sheltered lamp bracket, or rails ending at a wall. Most discoveries begin with walking past something interesting. Maps and coordinates are optional conveniences, not prerequisites for the story.

| Site | What the player finds | Practical reward | Narrative purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roadside Relief Shelter | Low roof, bench, hooks, weathered route board, a covered light niche | A finite bundle of ordinary travel supplies; a sample chalk bundle | A place where people waited and argued; proposed home for existing P1 |
| Bellwatch Post | Raised lookout, open bell frame, sight slots, bandage drawer | Light fittings and ordinary bandages in a finite cache | Teaches that the separate bell is not the storm's anchor; proposed P4 placement |
| Survey Cutting | Measuring marks, a collapsed cutting, portable braces, abandoned map case | Guide-line reel example and survey fittings | Suggests how measurements stopped agreeing; proposed P3 placement |
| Maintenance Yard | Sheltered work pit, damaged machine frames, small oil store | Modest ordinary salvage, a lantern-stake example, and decorative machinery | Connects practical machinery with modern danger; proposed P2 placement |
| Relief Store | Loading platform, empty ration shelves, coat pegs, worn notices | Cloth/twine, clothing finds, building pieces; ruined food is not a fresh-food reward | Shows the cost to waiting families and the failing relief effort |
| Lower Relay | A damaged maintenance shelter leading into a larger service complex | A finite major cache, a guaranteed Brace Maul reward, unique furnishing | Connects the four work stations; proposed P5/P6 dossier location and optional machinery encounter |
| Far Relay | A distant temporally disturbed isolation station | Proposed final-boss victory and guaranteed Closing Bell | An optional conclusion granting world-wide control over future Procession availability |

These placements develop the existing suggestions for P1–P6 and remain proposed. P5/P6 sit at the accessible shelter desk, so the machinery fight does not gate basic lore. Exact world frequency, generation rules, existing-world access, regional distribution, and loot quantities need separate decisions.

### The Lower Relay as an exploration chapter

The regional capstone is a compact place with a legible working layout and two approach routes. The player can understand where the crew ate, kept records, moved supplies, and maintained the apparatus. Seven spaces give it a beginning, a growing sense of failure, and a quiet end:

1. **The shelter desk.** Ordinary work papers and the P5/P6 dossier establish why someone stayed. One exit remains an obvious retreat route.
2. **The stores.** An emptied cupboard, ration tallies, and spare carrying slings suggest a long delay. Useful salvage sits among mundane loss.
3. **The helper bay.** Two damaged service machines demonstrate the shapes later echoed by the new locust variants. An optional Empty Stretcher encounter introduces movement along a narrow aisle.
4. **The brace gallery.** Heavy timber and mechanical stays support an ordinary passage. Cramped sightlines create tension; the player can walk through without solving a timed-collapse puzzle.
5. **The four stations.** Caller grille, helper controls, bracing assembly, and listening mount share a workspace. Empty chairs and tool wear suggest attendance without proving the identity of the present quartet.
6. **The machinery bay.** The separate Relief Engine blocks its own salvage area. A side passage permits exploration and departure without defeating it.
7. **The last landing.** A waiting bench, a damaged list with six entries, and a stopped route marker face a collapsed historical passage. Pell and Sella can remain legible; the other lines are damaged. Their fate stays unresolved.

The blocked historical passage is part of the old site, not a promise of a new dimension or a portal the player can reopen. The complex has ordinary terrain and physical routes; no impossible-space navigation system is required by this pitch.

### M9 — The Empty Stretcher

A low, jointed carrying machine moves around a wheeled frame with no passenger. It periodically aligns the frame with a clear aisle, rolls forward with a rattling burst, and stops hard at the end of its committed path.

**Tell:** the front wheels line up and two support arms lower. **Answer:** leave the aisle and strike its exposed side after the run. It turns poorly while committed, making the environment part of the fight. It does not capture, carry, or steal from the player.

Use small finite encounters in the helper bay or an appropriate maintenance-yard variant. It is a ruin enemy, not a ninth type added to the procession's reinforcement pool. Its salvage is modest, once per finite site placement; a defeated stretcher does not regenerate a cache.

### M10 — The Relief Engine

An optional miniboss built from a low hauling body, bracing feet, and a swinging recovery arm. It is a damaged machine encountered in a ruin, already present when discovered. Its appearance suggests a familiar tool doing its job dangerously; the player need not identify what, if anything, inhabits it.

The fight has three actions, each ending in a useful opening:

- **Set the feet:** the engine stops, braces, and sweeps a short arc with its arm. Move outside the arc or use the gallery's solid pillars.
- **Take the load:** it commits to one straight rolling push, scraping the ground before moving. Step aside; its slow reset exposes the rear assembly.
- **Clear the bay:** it pounds a small, clearly marked patch immediately in front of itself. Back away and return during the lifted-arm recovery.

As its casing breaks, its rhythms become irregular but its tells remain readable. Damaged machinery should feel more exposed, not suddenly faster at every action. There are no summoned adds, extra bells, chanters, storm locks, or immunity phases in this encounter.

Stopping the engine permits ordinary salvage of its own bay. The recommended finite reward is **one usable Brace Maul**, a modest repair/supply bundle, and one decorative relief maker's plate. The maul's ordinary-material crafting recipe remains available elsewhere; the machine supplies a finished example and a distinct trophy.

The engine is not the cause of the quartet, a mastermind, or a being summoned by their deaths. Defeating it does not end current or future temporal storms, close the mystery, or change the four's approved behavior. Its site does not reset as a renewable boss farm.

### M11 — The Last Custodian, a separate final boss

The user-requested endgame adds a distant Far Relay and an upright guardian, distinct from the squat Relief Engine. The proposed Custodian protects an isolation instrument. Its deliberate attacks gain delayed temporal echoes, then its failing frame advances from its station in the final act. Defeat grants access to the proposed Closing Bell, a placeable world-wide allowed/suppressed control.

Full site, combat, reward, operating-rule proposals, and unresolved decisions are in [ENDGAME_DESIGN.md](ENDGAME_DESIGN.md). The final boss is a separate expedition, not a storm-completion summon. The Engine remains an earlier miniboss, and a procession's last chanter still ends its current storm immediately.

## Progression without a separate leveling system

### First contact: understand enough to survive

A first heavy storm presents the directional buildup and four silhouettes. Accessible presentation communicates that the figures are sustaining the event. The player can attack, learn a tell, earn a source reward, or retreat. No prior ruin visit, recipe, unique item, or completed quest is required to hurt a chanter.

The current all-tier defender pool can make an early encounter severe. The expansion should describe that honestly rather than promise the event is safe for a particular metal tier. Its preparation suggestions are advice, not an entry gate.

### Field preparation: turn discoveries into a plan

The first shelter or yard gives an example of a field tool and enough context to make the kit understandable. Ordinary-material recipes appear in the handbook. Discoveries explain why someone might want a reel, fork, or light stake; they do not hide essential crafting instructions behind a random manuscript.

The player's next plan becomes concrete: carry a reliable melee weapon, bring recovery supplies, mark an escape line, and select an approach using cover. A solo player can prepare with craftable tools rather than waiting for a rare signature.

### Repeated storms: develop a preferred kit

Four source-specific relics provide the equipment chase. A bow user may prioritize the Veiled, while someone seeking the clasp targets the Hollow. The player balances a wanted drop against the easier approach and the benefits of completing the encounter.

The four chanters retain their independent proposed 15% signature rolls, including eligible partial kills. This expansion adds no pity rule, first-clear signature guarantee, relic fragments, or duplicate conversion. Those would be separate revisions of V5.

Full clears retain the proposed supply foundation, guaranteed dressing, and independent 20% lamp chance. New practical recipes and ruin finds make progress possible during an unlucky equipment streak; they do not secretly increase the signature odds.

### Investigation: follow people rather than a quest checklist

Existing P1–P4 offer separate sightings. P5 supplies the original instruction; P6 offers an explicitly uncertain interpretation. New minor records show the supply effort around them. Visiting all sites is optional, and none must be completed to end a storm.

P7–P8 remain **Resonance Archives-only**. The DLC adds no general-ruin copy, creature drop, trader copy, machine playback, crafting route, or substitute transcript. The Lower Relay can suggest their historical context without containing their testimony.

### A small ending: keep the names

After discovering the old list, the player can make an ordinary memorial board and choose an inscription. Suggested inscription: **Relief was sent. The names remain.**

This is a building and storytelling outcome. It supplies no permanent stat buff and does not prove the missing people were saved or the quartet released. The separate Far Relay expedition can now lead to earned world-wide event control; the memorial itself does not suppress the event. A player can close their investigation while the world remains uncertain.

### Final expedition: choose whether the call returns

The Lower Relay's separate route board points toward the distant isolation station. Defeating the final guardian earns world-wide control over whether the Procession appears. The proposed Closing Bell offers freely reversible allowed/suppressed modes while ordinary temporal storms continue. Exact timing, placement, loss recovery, and operation permissions remain under review. Rare signatures and Archives records are not required to reach this conclusion.

### Four cosmetic equipment pieces

**Relief coat:** a patched short work coat with a faded shoulder square. **Caller's scarf:** layered dark cloth with an ordinary stitched hem, not a magical veil. **Tender's apron:** a worn apron with asymmetric tool pockets. **Bracer's wraps:** weathered cloth stays adapted as clothing detail.

Use ordinary clothing categories where suitable; no new equipment-slot system is part of the pitch. Give each reasonable mundane wear/repair behavior and no combat set bonus. Craftable variants permit customization, while unusual recovered colorways make finite ruin finds interesting. Their resemblance to the quartet is suggestive, not proof that a particular individual wore them.

## More story, with room for uncertainty

Preserve all eight existing drafts in [LORE_COLLECTIBLES.md](LORE_COLLECTIBLES.md). New material should mainly show the people who supplied, watched, or waited for the operation. The quartet already has substantial testimony; surrounding lives make the loss feel larger without making the four explain themselves again.

Six proposed supplementary records are short, optional environmental texts. These excerpts are new drafts, not edits to P1–P8:

| ID / record | Proposed location | Draft excerpt | What it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 — Stores Correction | Relief store ledger | “The extra blankets are for the return party. They are not an error in the count. Leave them wrapped.” | Ordinary anticipation and resources reserved for people who never arrived |
| R2 — Notice of Relief | Roadside shelter notice | “Second shift: report to the lower shelter. Bring your own supper. Do not wait for the cart.” | A deteriorating operation, understood through a mundane instruction |

The notices establish local witnesses and circumstances only. They cannot diagnose what became of the missing people, prove that every machine was alive, or explain the storm's rules as a universal law. Any future final prose should sound like a specific person writing for a practical reason.

An optional field-journal page can collect discovered titles and player observations. It records facts such as “four figures,” “the survivors recovered,” and “the storm ended when the last fell.” Interpretations are marked as possibilities. It awards no XP and does not require a reading order.

A proposed journal summary after reading the dossier:

> The instruction was to keep the way open until the lower party answered. The four still appear to be doing that work. I cannot tell whether they know what is coming through.

This is player interpretation, not an omniscient final reveal. The journal must not reproduce an undiscovered Archives-only transcript.

## Bring the rescue home

Ten furnishing families turn exploration into a lasting change to the player's home:

| Family | Useful expression |
|---|---|
| Guide-posts and arrow plates | Route marking and settlement wayfinding |
| Hooded light brackets | Floor/wall fittings for appropriate ordinary lights |
| Timber brace pieces | Workshop and mine-support appearance; structural behavior remains ordinary unless separately designed |
| Weathered grilles | Window, vent, and partition decoration |
| Oil-cup shelves | Small storage/display detail for a work area |
| Ration boards | Writable or selectable ordinary notices, with no resource-generating function |
| Empty stretcher stands | A recognizable display piece assembled from recovered fittings |
| Inert station consoles | Four visual workstations for a maintenance room; decorative, not advanced functional machinery |
| Memorial boards | Optional player inscription with the recovered six-entry list as a suggested motif |
| Relief maker's plates | A finite engine trophy and craftable plain variants for workshop decoration |

Most pieces should have ordinary-material crafting alternatives so the expansion also rewards builders. Distinct recovered wear patterns can make ruin salvage special. Valuable machinery components in V5 do not become freely extractable from every decorative console; furnishing salvage and material payouts need explicit separate treatment.

### Small environmental details

At a cleared procession site, fading impressions briefly show where the four stood. Their arrangement matches the encounter's formation. These are visual traces that disappear, not permanent terrain edits or a second loot field.

An abandoned shelter can have four differently worn work surfaces rather than four labeled exposition plaques. A coat peg is rubbed smooth. A ration board has a clean rectangle where a notice was removed. One guide arrow points toward a passage now closed by ordinary collapse.

In snow, the emerging forms leave a short, readable track from the rift; in grass, stems bend around their feet. Such details belong to arrival presentation and should not become a tracking requirement or an extra spawning system.

Ambient dust and intermittent mechanical settling give ruins movement without adding a population of disposable ambient creatures. Clear distinction between an actual enemy tell and background creaking keeps the soundscape useful.

## Art and sound direction

Retain the cuboid anatomy, pixel-scale material detail, and four silhouettes in [VISUALS.md](VISUALS.md). The Hollow's head is the revised **open U**, not the closed rectangular frame in the old concept images.

The material palette is charcoal cloth, dirty linen, timber darkened by handling, rusted iron, and restrained verdigris. Teal belongs to small seams and important moving parts. Faded ochre and chalk-white belong to ordinary relief signs. Bright effects should be reserved for a meaningful state change.

Keep the four figures identifiable at poor visibility: high hood, wide hunch, cage head, and open fork. New defenders borrow secondary details, such as carrying rails and shutters, rather than copying these complete outlines. The boss is low and industrial, giving it a different profile from all four.

The 15–30-second arrival buildup develops from a far mechanical vibration into four distinguishable parts. During combat, the bell and incoming-defender cues need space around those parts. Reduce decorative audio when necessary so the player can hear an actual attack commitment.

The final lone chanter should sound exposed: the same part continuing without the others. Its death is followed by a brief release of tension and the ordinary environment becoming audible. That tonal change is the emotional finish; a second combat crescendo would weaken it.

Make the crucial information available through multiple channels: attack posture, distinct sound, optional directional captions, and a clear heal cue. Support reduced flashing and independently adjustable chant/effect volume. These are baseline presentation goals, not equipment perks.

## Reward economy across the whole DLC

Three sources have different purposes:

| Activity | Proposed reward character | Why repeat or return? |
|---|---|---|
| Eligible chanter kills and full storm clears | V5's finite source rolls, supply payouts, completion dressing/lamp | Valuable routine supplies plus four lasting equipment goals |
| Ordinary-material crafting | Field kit, food, clothes, and most furnishings | Reliable preparation and expression without depending on a rare roll |
| Ruin exploration and machinery defeat | Finite caches, equipment examples, records, unique wear patterns, trophy, and separate final control reward | New places, context, a substantial finished-item find, and an optional earned endgame |

For this DLC proposal, carry forward V5's recommendation that replenishing event defenders provide **no material drops, harvest yield, or stability recovery**, with bounded one-time bell salvage. This remains a recommendation awaiting review. New special defenders do not add a private token table or a source of unlimited parts.

Finite ruin machines can have modest designated salvage because the proposed site encounters do not replenish. Their caches and the engine's maul are claimed once per generated site, with no loot reset on return. Multiple discovered sites are legitimate exploration rewards; that is distinct from farming one machine indefinitely.

The DLC does not claim to solve ordinary vanilla-mob farming during a prolonged storm. Those creatures remain outside the event cap, and their economics remain an open balance issue in the canonical questions.

Four source-specific 15% rolls are still four independent chances, not a guaranteed item or a 60% shared pool. Extra content should never be described as proof that those odds feel fair at an unmeasured storm frequency. The craftable kit, finite finds, and ordinary supplies provide value alongside that uncertainty.

## How a session could feel

This is an illustrative player story, not a claimed time budget or gameplay test.

You find a low shelter on a trip for other supplies. A faded board reserves blankets for a return party. Inside is chalk, some ordinary travel salvage, and a journal describing a voice in the rain.

Later, a heavy storm begins. You recognize the directional buildup and put a recovery crate under a familiar overhang. A short guide line marks the way back. You approach with your existing weapon and healing supplies; none of the new kit is mandatory.

The quartet stands in a crescent. A Linekeeper delays the direct approach, while a bowtorn covers the open bank. You cross behind a rock, kill a dangerous defender, and use the shared spawn pause to reach the nearer chanter. When it falls, the remaining three visibly recover and the combined chant loses a voice.

A Rackback arrives after the shared pause and normal pacing. You hold cover for its whole volley, then cross during the reset. The second approach is a choice: finish another chanter, secure a dropped source reward, or retreat before stability becomes too dangerous.

On a successful clear, the last voice stops and the storm ends immediately. Ordinary completion supplies arrive under V5's proposed rules; the bell need not be dead. The fate of any surviving guards still follows the separate lifecycle rule once that is settled, so this pitch does not promise an instantly safe loot window.

Between storms, the work records lead you to the Lower Relay. You read the old instruction before deciding whether to face the machine in the side bay. Beyond the four empty stations is a bench facing a blocked passage and a list with six places.

Back home, you fit a light bracket above your workshop and put the list beside it. Another heavy storm will come. You have better tools and a clearer idea of what the figures might be waiting for.

Later, the route board can lead to the Far Relay and its Custodian. After that expedition, a proposed Closing Bell beside the memorial lets you choose whether the next heavy storm brings the quartet at all. The storm itself still comes.

## Design review of the proposal

The strongest additions are the readable defender roles, the network of practical ruins, and equipment that brings the rescue theme into survival play. They give the expansion both a combat identity and a reason to exist between storms.

The likely excesses need conscious restraint:

| Tension | Recommended treatment |
|---|---|
| Eight new defender variants could overload a small encounter | Start the concept around the two-special composition target, strong recovery windows, and ordinary family members retaining a major role |
| A field kit could turn every storm into lengthy setup | All preparation tools stay optional; a familiar weapon, supplies, and terrain remain a valid approach |
| Two kinds of portable light could feel redundant | Stakes mark a route for minutes; flares briefly reveal an approach; the rare lamp remains a permanent everyday light |
| More records could explain away the mystery | New scraps concentrate on waiting, supplies, and ordinary work; P7/P8 keep their exclusive perspective and location |
| A boss could displace the quartet as the expansion's real point | The Engine is a separate miniboss and the Custodian an optional distant conclusion; the four-part storm fight remains the recurring centerpiece when allowed |
| Eleven creature concepts could feel like unrelated monsters | Carrying, guiding, bracing, illuminating, and isolating connect their silhouettes and behavior without asserting common canonical origins |
| Powerful rewards could trivialize later storms | Keep V5's bounded effects; give new tools specific jobs and avoid stacking new stability resistance or universal damage bonuses |

If a defender cannot be distinguished by silhouette, attack tell, and player response, merge it into another variant rather than keep it for the creature count. If a tool is rarely worth carrying, improve its everyday use or fold its function into an existing item. These are quality criteria for the concept, not an implementation schedule.

### Decisions still needed

The next review should choose the expansion's tone and favorite content, then settle the new combat behaviors, two-special composition rule, field-tool functions, and ruin progression. Exact recipes, loot quantities, gear statistics, and world frequency follow those choices.

Existing unresolved core questions remain in [OPEN_QUESTIONS.md](OPEN_QUESTIONS.md): family shutdown across sources, bell child pool, initial filling toward eight, shared-pause edge cases, placement, guard survival after victory, despawn/reload recovery, stability scope, and multiplayer. This document deliberately does not answer them through incidental fiction.

The expansion's new accounting questions include finite ruin claims, separation of static ruin enemies from procession ownership, storage/line recovery, item repair and stacking, and repeat discoveries. They are future design and verification topics. Documentation review cannot establish playable behavior or compatibility.
